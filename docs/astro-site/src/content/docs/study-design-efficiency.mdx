---
title: Study-design efficiency
description: Experimental COSS, signed ENBS, EVSI/EVPI, and constrained portfolio contracts
---

The experimental study-design family evaluates a declared finite set of
designs without extrapolating beyond it. `calculate_coss` returns the full
EVSI, research-cost, and signed-ENBS curve, feasibility set or range,
deterministic ties and optimum, boundary state, uncertainty, diagnostics,
provenance, and plotting vectors in `CossResultV1`.

`evsi_evpi_efficiency` is a dimensionless diagnostic. Its EVSI and EVPI inputs
must describe the same decision problem, units, population scale, horizon, and
discounting. The contract reports numerical-zero EVPI explicitly and diagnoses
tolerance-sized bounds excursions; it does not clamp materially invalid values
or reuse the legacy value-to-cost `voi_efficiency` label.

The initial portfolio surface performs exact subset enumeration over governed
single-study COSS optima. It maximizes additive signed ENBS, permits the empty
portfolio, and enforces declared capacity, dependency, exclusion, and guardrail
constraints. Each candidate must also declare primary and secondary metrics,
heterogeneous and delayed-effect models, interference, multiplicity,
sequential monitoring, stopping rules, duration, opportunity cost,
implementation delay, and the expected policy change. The result preserves
those declarations and reports gross and net EVSI/ENBS separately. VOIAGE does
not invent domain-specific adjustment estimates when the caller has not
supplied them, and experiment-platform adapters remain optional.

Rust owns the numerical COSS and efficiency policy, while Python provides the
strict experimental contracts, orchestration, CLI, reporting, and accessible
plot. The retained R and Julia bindings report the capability as unsupported;
Mojo remains an external boundary. No stable or full-polyglot parity claim is
made before scientific review, installed conformance evidence, merge, and
release gates are separately satisfied.

See the [CLI reference](/cli-reference/#experimental-coss-workflow) for a
reproducible example.
